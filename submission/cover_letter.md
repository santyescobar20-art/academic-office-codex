Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript "Crackdown or Relocation? Targeted States of Exception and the Geography of Lethal Violence in Ecuador, 2024–2026" for publication as an original article.

Since 2024 Ecuador has confronted the steepest rise in homicide in Latin America with emergency decrees that place named provinces and cantons under military-assisted policing. We reconstruct the territorial and temporal scope of all 24 decrees, link them to record-level homicide data for 221 cantons, and estimate effects in covered cantons and in the uncovered cantons that border them.

We think the paper fits the journal for two reasons. It follows directly from Escaño and Pridemore's recent article in JQC on military intervention and homicide in Mexican municipalities, and extends that line of work to spatial spillovers. And its main message is methodological: the decrees are issued after short-lived surges, so that a pooled two-way fixed-effects model, an event study normalised on the eve of entry, and a specification that sets the surge aside give estimates of +40%, −47% and zero from the same data. We report the direct effect as bounds under explicit assumptions, bring a benchmark from earlier surges and an exit analysis to bear on them, and show that the spillover design, unlike the direct one, passes its pre-trend test.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All data are public and the replication files accompany the submission. The text is about 5,300 words excluding references, with four tables and four figures.
